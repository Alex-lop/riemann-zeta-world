Zeta World

Ground: s = σ + it. Height: log(1 + |ζ(s)|). Color: the phase (angle) of ζ(s). The white line is the critical line σ = ½. Green dots are zeros.

The same ground and coloring, now for Riemann's completed function ξ(s). It has exactly the same zeros as ζ in the strip, but its landscape is a mirror image across the white line.

How ξ relates to ζ ξ(s) = ½ · s(s−1) · π−s/2 · Γ(s/2) · ζ(s)

Each extra factor cancels something in ζ:

  • s − 1 cancels the pole of ζ at s = 1 (the spike in the ζ tab).
  • Γ(s/2) has poles at s = 0, −2, −4, … They cancel the trivial zeros of ζ at −2, −4, …, and the factor s cancels the pole at 0.
  • π−s/2Γ(s/2) is the factor that turns ζ(s) = χ(s)·ζ(1−s) into a clean symmetry.

What is left has no poles, and its zeros are exactly the nontrivial zeros of ζ. It satisfies

ξ(s) = ξ(1−s)  and  ξ(s̄) = conj ξ(s)

Together: ξ(1−s̄) = conj ξ(s). The point 1−s̄ is the mirror image of s across σ = ½, so |ξ| is mirror-symmetric, and on the line itself ξ is real. That is why the white line shows only two colors: positive and negative, switching at each zero.

Scaling. |ξ(½+it)| shrinks like e−πt/4 (about 10−31 at t = 100), so the terrain plots ξ̃ = ξ(s) / N(t), where N(t) is the size of the extra factors at ½ + it. N(t) is positive and does not depend on σ, so the zeros, the colors and the mirror symmetry are unchanged. Along the white line |ξ̃| = |ζ|, so both tabs show the same height there. In fact ξ̃(½ + it) = −Z(t), Hardy's function.
