Zeta World

The Riemann hypothesis, as a place you can look at.

This landscape is the size of the Riemann zeta function, which encodes how the primes are spread out. Each pin stands on a zero, the first 100 of infinitely many. The Riemann hypothesis says they all sit on the gold line.

Status, 22 Nov 2026: a computer-checked proof of the Riemann hypothesis was released on 20 Nov and certified correct on 21 Nov in a joint statement of the Lean and Mathlib communities. Its author has published it only as Lean code, with no paper yet, and it has not been peer reviewed. This page shows what the statement says. It proves nothing.

Loading the 3D view. It needs JavaScript and WebGL.

How to read it Height is the size of ζ(s), squashed so the infinite pole fits: (2/π) atan |ζ(s)|. The land covers Re(s) from −0.5 to 1.5 and Im(s) from 0 to 237, and the whole plane, when shown, Re(s) from −9.5 to 4.5 and Im(s) from −48 to 48. Shade is height too: the darker the land, the lower it is. The gold line is Re(s) = ½, the middle of the critical strip (Re(s) from 0 to 1), which holds every nontrivial zero. The grey lines are the strip’s edges, and the numbers along the east side are heights, Im(s). The mound at the south end is the pole at s = 1, where ζ is infinite.
