Zeta World

The Riemann hypothesis, as a place you can look at.

This landscape is the size of the Riemann zeta function, which encodes how the prime numbers are spread out. Each pin stands on a zero, where the land drops to height zero. These are the first 13 of infinitely many. The Riemann hypothesis says they all sit on the gold line.

Posed in 1859. Trillions of zeros have been checked by computer and every one is on the line. This page shows what the hypothesis says. It proves nothing.

Loading the 3D view. It needs JavaScript and WebGL.

How to read it Height is |ζ(s)| over the critical strip, Re(s) from 0 to 1, drawn for Im(s) from 0 to 60. The hypothesis is about the zeros inside this strip. Color is the phase of ζ(s): cyan where it is positive, red where it is negative. The gold line is Re(s) = ½. The cut-off spike at one end is the pole at s = 1, where ζ is infinite.
